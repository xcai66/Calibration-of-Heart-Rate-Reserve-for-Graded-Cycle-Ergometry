# Research canon

## Tier 1: direct conceptual and data foundations

1. Schumann M, et al. Using Free-Living Heart Rate Data as an Objective Method to Assess Physical Activity: A Scoping Review and Recommendations by the INTERLIVE-Network Targeting Consumer Wearables. Sports Medicine. 2025;55:275-300. doi:10.1007/s40279-024-02159-1.
   - Defines the 10% HRR-cluster activity-score framework extended here.
   - Supports individualized HRR normalization and reporting of HRR distributions.

2. Donne B, Fleming N, Campbell G, Ward T, Crampton D, Mahony N. Graded Incremental Test Data (Cycling, Running, Kayaking, Rowing): an open access dataset. Zenodo, version 2. 2024. doi:10.5281/zenodo.10841412.
   - Development and temporal-holdout source.
   - Provides stage-level HR, VO2, lactate, and workload across 835 graded tests.
   - CC BY 4.0; original source reports ethics approval and consent.

3. Chabert C, Mongin D, Hermand E, Collado A, Hue O. Cardiorespiratory measurement from graded cycloergometer exercise testing. PhysioNet, version 1.0.0. 2022. doi:10.13026/2qs3-kh43.
   - Independent external-validation source.
   - Provides beat-to-beat RR intervals, oxygen consumption, and power for 18 maximal cycling tests.

## Tier 2: HRR and metabolic-intensity evidence

4. Swain DP, Leutholtz BC. Heart rate reserve is equivalent to %VO2 reserve, not to %VO2max. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise. 1997;29:410-414. doi:10.1097/00005768-199703000-00018.
   - Historical basis for relating HRR to VO2R.

5. Lounana J, Campion F, Noakes TD, Medelli J. Relationship between %HRmax, %HR reserve, %VO2max, and %VO2 reserve in elite cyclists. Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise. 2007;39:350-357. doi:10.1249/01.MSS.0000246996.63976.5F.
   - Cycling-specific evidence that HRR is more closely aligned with VO2R than with VO2max.

6. Ferri Marini C, et al. HRR and VO2R Fractions Are Not Equivalent: Is It Time to Rethink Aerobic Exercise Prescription Methods? Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise. 2021;53:174-182. doi:10.1249/MSS.0000000000002434.
   - Large-sample evidence against universal 1:1 equivalence.

7. Olsson KSE, Rosdahl H, Schantz P. Interchangeability and optimization of heart rate methods for estimating oxygen uptake in ergometer cycling, level treadmill walking and running. BMC Medical Research Methodology. 2022;22:55. doi:10.1186/s12874-022-01524-w.
   - Supports activity-mode-specific calibration and wider intensity ranges.

8. Cunha FA, Midgley AW, Monteiro WD, Campos FK, Farinatti PTV. The relationship between oxygen uptake reserve and heart rate reserve is affected by intensity and duration during aerobic exercise at constant work rate. Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism. 2011;36:839-847. doi:10.1139/h11-100.
   - Establishes duration and intensity as boundaries of HRR-VO2R interchangeability.

9. Ferri Marini C, et al. Effect of steady-state aerobic exercise intensity and duration on the relationship between reserves of heart rate and oxygen uptake. PeerJ. 2022;10:e13190. doi:10.7717/peerj.13190.
   - Reinforces duration-dependent divergence.

10. Zoladz JA, Duda K, Majerczak J. Oxygen uptake does not increase linearly at high power outputs during incremental exercise test in humans. European Journal of Applied Physiology. 1998;77:445-451. doi:10.1007/s004210050358.
   - Provides physiological context for a high-intensity deviation from simple linearity.

## Tier 3: wearable deployment and construct boundaries

11. Mühlen JM, et al. Recommendations for determining the validity of consumer wearable heart rate devices: expert statement and checklist of the INTERLIVE Network. British Journal of Sports Medicine. 2021;55:767-779. doi:10.1136/bjsports-2020-103148.
   - Requires device-, activity-, and intensity-specific validation.

12. Zhang Y, Weaver RG, Armstrong B, Burkart S, Zhang S, Beets MW. Validity of wrist-worn photoplethysmography devices to measure heart rate: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Journal of Sports Sciences. 2020;38:2021-2034. doi:10.1080/02640414.2020.1767348.
   - Shows activity-dependent wrist-HR error and a larger mean difference during cycling than at rest/treadmill activity.

13. Impellizzeri FM, Marcora SM, Coutts AJ. Internal and External Training Load: 15 Years On. International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance. 2019;14:270-273. doi:10.1123/ijspp.2018-0935.
   - Prevents conflating HR response, external power, and the training-load construct.

14. Klass M, Faoro V, Carpentier A. Assessment of energy expenditure during high intensity cycling and running using a heart rate and activity monitor in young active adults. PLOS ONE. 2019;14:e0224948. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0224948.
   - Supports the need for activity- and individual-aware calibration when heart rate is used to estimate energetic demand at higher intensities.
   - Does not establish the present transfer function or a universal high-intensity correction.

## Citation rule

Each reference must be verified against a publisher page, PubMed, Crossref, DataCite, Zenodo, or PhysioNet before finalization. The manuscript must not cite a source for a claim that the source does not directly support.
